# Scenario: writing and research

Result — an article, review, documentation, talk, post, source research. Typical statement: "write an article about X", "review the solutions", "prepare a talk".

## Analysis hints

**Point 1 — goal and bars.** Text is a means: what should the reader understand, decide, or do, and what stops them now? Baseline bar — text per the brief, with checked facts and a reader-oriented structure. Ambitious — usually: say what existing texts on the topic don't (new fact, primary source, your own check of a claim); let the reader act, not just understand; question and check a widely held claim on the topic. For a complex topic the ambitious bar is a learning goal: "find out whether the main thesis holds up against primary sources".

**Gate check.** Source reconnaissance: are there primary sources (data, documents, interviews), or does the topic live on retellings? Main-thesis check: does a 30-minute search find confirmation or refutation? Criterion — observable: "≥2 primary sources found", "thesis confirmed / refuted / unclear".

**Point 2 — improvements.** Assumption to check: that the audience doesn't know what is in the brief (maybe it does — and the text isn't needed); that the topic's accepted fact is true. Disciplines: journalism (primary source, two independent confirmations, direct quote with context); editing (structure for skimming; one idea per paragraph); analytics (number with source and date); legal review for public texts about people and companies.

**Point 3 — grounds.** Primary: original documents, data, recordings — not reviews of reviews. For documentation — the code or product itself, not old docs. For a talk — audience questions from past talks. Worker's tools: search and source access; a way to verify quotes; a draft format that shows structure.

**Point 4 — verification.** Criteria: every factual claim has a source the verifier can open; an audience reader performs the text's task (answers a control question, takes an action) — checked on 2–3 people or an agent in a reader role given only the text and question; brief compliance against a list. Independent verification: the verifier opens sources selectively; a reader agent on another model answers control questions on the text. Falsification test: the main thesis — is there an authoritative source contradicting it, and is it mentioned?

## Traps

- **Confidence without a source.** A claim "everyone knows". Either a source or the wording "it is believed that".
- **Text for the author.** Structure follows research logic, not the reader's questions. Check — does the reader find their answer in 30 seconds?
- **Review without a position.** "On the one hand, on the other hand". If the brief asks for a conclusion — there must be one, with grounds.
- **Gaming the length.** N words is not a goal. The criterion "the reader does X" outweighs "3000 words".

## For the worker

- Start with the source check, if section 4 has it; the list of primary sources found goes into the log before the draft.
- Every factual claim — a link to its source in the log. A claim without a source — a flag in the text and under "unverified".
- Structure follows the reader's questions: headings answer questions, the first paragraph of each section gives the answer.
- Main thesis — find the best argument against it and answer it in the text, or honestly say there is no answer.
- Control questions for the reader — write 3, pass to the verifier separately from the text.
- Hand over: the text; a list of sources stating which claim each supports; control questions; a list of unverified claims.

## Example of two bars

Task: "Write an article on how AI agents are changing a designer's work."

- Baseline: an article per the brief with checked sources, structure built on reader questions, control questions.
- Ambitious: find primary data (surveys, job postings, tool usage logs) that confirms or refutes the popular thesis "designers will no longer be needed"; check — within 1 hour find ≥2 primary sources with data from the last 12 months (limit — 1 hour; not found → baseline with the thesis "no data, here is what exists").
